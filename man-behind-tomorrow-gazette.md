**Jakes Olivier — The Man Behind Tomorrow Gazette**

Technologist by trade, futurist by curiosity, and collector of ideas that occasionally escape containment. Jakes is the South African mind behind *Tomorrow Gazette*, a retro-futuristic adventure revisiting yesterday's predictions of tomorrow and checking them against today's reality. He's combining decades of technology experience with AI, science, humour, and a healthy suspicion of anyone who promises us flying cars by next Tuesday.

**The future is weird enough. Let's investigate.**
</aside>

Some people spend their lives predicting the future. Others spend their lives trying to build it. Jakes Olivier has somehow managed to get himself involved in both — which probably explains a lot.

Based in South Africa, Jakes is a technology enthusiast, lifelong learner, incurable idea collector, and firm believer that the universe is far too fascinating to spend your entire life staring at spreadsheets. Although, in fairness, he has spent a fair amount of time staring at spreadsheets.

With nearly three decades in technology, he's seen enough promises of revolutionary breakthroughs to know that the future rarely arrives exactly as advertised. Artificial intelligence, automation, software engineering, and the occasional existential question over a cup of coffee are all part of the territory.

But somewhere between wondering where humanity is headed and asking why we still haven't perfected the flying car, an idea emerged: **What if we could revisit yesterday's vision of tomorrow — and see how it held up?**

Enter *Tomorrow Gazette*.

Part retro-futuristic newsreel, part science and technology reality check, and part affectionate roast of humanity's boundless optimism, the show dusts off predictions from decades ago and puts them to the test of modern reality. Our fictional future-facing presenter, Vera, does the honours, while the rest of us get to marvel at the things we got right, the things we got spectacularly wrong, and the things we're still somehow working on.

Jakes is building more than a YouTube channel. He's experimenting with what happens when curiosity meets artificial intelligence, a little creative mischief, and the conviction that learning should be entertaining. Think vintage visions of tomorrow, modern-day science, a healthy dose of scepticism, and absolutely no requirement to take ourselves too seriously.

When he's not dreaming up new projects, exploring the possibilities of AI, or asking questions that probably need their own filing system, he's interested in the bigger picture: how technology can help people, how good ideas become useful things, and how we might leave the world a little better than we found it.

He's also a fan of the wonderfully improbable — because the best ideas often start with someone saying, *"This is probably ridiculous, but hear me out..."*

## Why support Tomorrow Gazette?

Because the future belongs to all of us, and understanding it shouldn't require a PhD, a corporate subscription, or the patience of a medieval monk.

The ambition is simple: build something that makes people curious, makes them laugh, and occasionally makes them stop and think.
